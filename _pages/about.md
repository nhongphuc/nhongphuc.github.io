I am a quantum physicist and researcher with a strong foundation in **quantum information theory**, **quantum error correction**, and **quantum gravity**. My work sits at the intersection of theoretical physics, quantum computing, and computational methods, where I focus on understanding how quantum information behaves in complex systems—and how to leverage that understanding for fault-tolerant quantum technologies.

I was trained as a physicist and earned my Ph.D. in Physics from the University of Texas at Austin. My research spans quantum information theory, quantum chaos, tensor networks, and quantum error correcting codes—including approximate QEC schemes derived from gravitational holography. Over time, I've expanded my research to include computational and data science applications, where I apply ideas from quantum information and statistical physics to real-world problems involving complex, high-dimensional data. I currently work as a Data Scientist at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston, developing generative models and information-theoretic tools for biomedical applications.

My core technical expertise includes:

**Quantum Computing & Physics**

- Quantum error correction (approximate codes, stabilizer codes, tensor network approaches)
- Quantum information theory, entanglement structure, and information spreading/scrambling
- Holographic duality and gravitational physics connections to quantum codes

**Computational & ML Methods**

- Python, numerical simulation, MCMC methods
- Generative models, deep neural networks, probabilistic modeling
- Building and publishing open-source Python packages (sentropy, lucie)
  
I maintain a broad range of interests across physics, from particle physics and cosmology to general relativity and quantum computing. I actively develop and maintain open-source Python tools.

I am currently seeking opportunities to contribute as a research scientist or postdoctoral fellow in quantum computing and/or machine learning.

Feel free to explore my publications, projects, and code, or reach out if you'd like to collaborate.
